NEEDS-PATCH

1. **Bimodal Blindness / Variance Risk**: By requiring both mean $\ge 0.70$ and median $\ge 0.70$, the test becomes hypersensitive to a "left-tail" of a few catastrophic failures. In a bimodal distribution where 60% of runs are elite (0.90) and 40% are mediocre (0.60), the median (0.75) and mean (0.78) both pass, but the "typical" run is actually a coin-flip between excellence and failure. 

2. **Median Gaming**: Because the median is the average of the 20th and 21st sorted values, a researcher can "save" a failing run-set by marginally boosting just 20 runs to $\ge 0.70$, even if the other 20 are near-zero. This creates a "cliff" effect where the mean provides a safety check, but the median allows for high-variance instability as long as the 50th percentile is barely cleared.
