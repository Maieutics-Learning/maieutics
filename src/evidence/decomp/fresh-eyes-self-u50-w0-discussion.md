"The blood was still wet on the ledger when the auditor arrived."

Elias didn't look up from the book. He didn't need to. He knew the sound of a man who had spent twenty years pretending to be blind to the gore of the slaughterhouse as long as the numbers balanced. 

The auditor, a skeletal man named Vane, stepped over a coil of severed intestines with a practiced, rhythmic avoidance, as if navigating a familiar sidewalk. He didn't comment on the smell of copper and bile; in the Low District, scent was merely a geography of status.

"You're late," Elias said, his voice rasping. He finally looked up, his eyes milky with cataracts, yet somehow seeing right through Vane.

"The tide was high," Vane replied, tapping a gold-tipped cane against the stone floor. "And the bridges are currently occupied by the grieving. I assume the quota was met?"

Elias pointed a stained finger toward the pits. Three massive, grey-skinned things lay sprawled in the muck, their chests carved open to reveal hearts that pulsed with a slow, rhythmic bioluminescence. They weren't dead—not quite—but they were processed.

"Three Wave-Zeros," Elias whispered. "Clean cuts. No wastage."

Vane leaned in, squinting at the iridescent organs. He didn't check for quality; he checked for the mark. There, etched into the cartilage of the thoracic cavity, were the twin marks of the 9th cycle. 

"The twins," Vane murmured, a flicker of genuine curiosity crossing his face. "I was told they'd be impossible to harvest."

"Nothing is impossible," Elias said, finally closing the ledger with a heavy thud. "Only expensive."

Vane stepped back, his expression shifting back to the cold mask of bureaucracy. He reached into his coat and produced a small, obsidian coin—the payment for the labor, and the receipt for the soul of the man who had done the carving. 

He placed the coin on the blood-slicked table. Elias didn't touch it. He simply watched as the coin began to sink, not through the wood, but through the very fabric of the table, as if the furniture itself were dissolving into a void.

"The price has gone up," Vane noted.

"The cost of living," Elias replied, "is a bitch."
