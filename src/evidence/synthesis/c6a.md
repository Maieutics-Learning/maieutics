## LIMA (Zhou et al., Meta, 2305.11206) — USED (methodology license)
65B LLaMA + 1,000 curated demos (750 forum Q-A sampled for quality and
diversity + 250 hand-written for task diversity + uniform style). Beats
RLHF DaVinci003 and 52k-example Alpaca in human preference; 88% meet
requirements, 50% excellent.
Quotes (w18-lima.txt):
- "fine-tuned with the standard supervised loss on only 1,000 carefully
  curated prompts and responses, without any reinforcement learning or
  human preference modeling."
- "We select 750 top questions and answers from community forums ...
  sampling for quality and diversity ... manually write 250 examples ...
  optimizing for task diversity and emphasizing a uniform response
  style."
- "Ablation experiments reveal vastly diminishing returns when scaling
  up data quantity without also scaling up prompt diversity, alongside
  major gains when optimizing data quality."
- Thesis: "We define the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis: A model's
  knowledge and capabilities are learnt almost entirely during
  pretraining, while alignment teaches it which subdistribution of
  formats should be used."
Novelty delta: they [align via 1k curated SFT] under [65B pretrained
model]; we [ignite lookup from zero across generations] under [tiny
students]; claim holds — and the contrast sharpens us: Superficial
Alignment says fine-tuning teaches FORMAT; our loop teaches a CAPABILITY
(lookup ignition). State explicitly so no reviewer files us under
"style-tuning." So-what: less-is-more + diversity-over-quantity licenses
our tiny hand-designed nursery rows; our failure-repertoire diversity IS
the diversity axis.
Placement: PAPER (methodology paragraph, RW-LIMA) + NURSERY (curate +
diversify rows, don't scale blindly). No new experiment.

## TinyStories (Eldan & Li, MSR, 2305.07759) — USED (regime precedent)
Synthetic stories in 3–4-year-old vocabulary (GPT-3.5/4-generated);
sub-10M models, even single-block, produce fluent consistent
multi-paragraph stories with near-perfect grammar; GPT-4-as-teacher
grading paradigm.
Quotes (w17-tiny.txt):
- "we introduce TinyStories, a synthetic dataset of short stories that
  only contain words that a typical 3 to 4-year-olds usually understand,
  generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4."
- "train and evaluate LMs that are much smaller than the
  state-of-the-art models (below 10 million total parameters), or have
  much simpler architectures (with only one transformer block), yet
  still produce fluent and consistent stories."
- "demonstrate reasoning capabilities" + "almost perfect grammar."
- Eval paradigm: "uses GPT-4 to grade the content generated by these
  models as if those were stories written by students and graded by a
  (human) teacher."
Novelty delta: they [constrain domain so tiny models learn] under
[story pretraining]; we [constrain task so tiny models ignite] under
[lookup curriculum]; claim holds — same regime philosophy, different
object (data recipe vs generational teaching). So-what: precedent that
small+synthetic works + OUR contrast (computed metric gates Hd/killA,
zero API cost, harder than GPT-4-judge).
Placement: PAPER (regime precedent, RW-TINY) + NURSERY (philosophy
affirmed; gates already superior). No new experiment.

## Pruning/coresets (Toneva forgetting 1812.05159; Paul EL2N 2107.07075) — USED (selection vocabulary)
Forgetting event = correct→incorrect transition mid-training; some
examples forgotten constantly, some never; (un)forgettable sets
generalize across architectures; large fractions omittable. EL2N/GraNd
scores averaged over inits identify important examples a few epochs in;
prune half of CIFAR-10 while slightly improving accuracy.
Quotes:
- Forgetting: "We define a 'forgetting event' to have occurred when an
  individual training example transitions from being classified
  correctly to incorrectly over the course of learning." (w19-forget.txt)
- "We will call the latter unforgettable examples" — "examples that are
  never forgotten." (w19-forget.txt)
- "a data set's (un)forgettable examples generalize across neural
  architectures" + "a significant fraction of examples can be omitted
  from the training data set while still maintaining state-of-the-art
  generalization performance." (w19-forget.txt)
- EL2N: "using EL2N scores calculated a few epochs into training, we
  can prune half of the CIFAR10 training set while slightly improving
  test accuracy." (w19-el2n.txt)
Novelty delta: they [select training EXAMPLES by forgetting/gradient
scores] under [supervised training]; we [select TUTORS by
winner-promotion and probe the IGNITION CORE by severance] under
[generational loop]; claim holds — convergent selection principle,
different object (examples vs tutors). So-what: pruning vocabulary names
both ends of our loop (ignition core = unforgettable; failure repertoire
= most-forgotten profile → F-skillbank); early-score selection (EL2N
few-epochs-in) parallels tutor selection by early signal.
Placement: PAPER (one-line convergent cite, RW-PRUNE) + NURSERY
(F-skillbank mistake-profile selection keeps its pruning-vocabulary
framing). No new experiment.

## Dataset distillation — SKIPPED with teeth (tag: no-fit/cost-mismatch)
Method (scout-level): compress datasets into tiny synthetic sets via
gradient/trajectory matching — requires unrolled training trajectories +
bilevel optimization, i.e. SOAR-grade compute against a 50-free-OR/day,
30-T4h/week budget. A method that costs more than the training it
replaces is definitionally out of a cheap-teaching program. SKIP stands
unless budget changes 10×. (First SKIP tag of this kind; no 3-of-a-kind.)
No quotes needed — verdict rests on method-knowledge cost arithmetic,
not on paper claims.
