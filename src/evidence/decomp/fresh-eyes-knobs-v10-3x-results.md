## Ask
Verdict on design / hardest flaw or missing control / most surprising block.

## Raw replies (verbatim)
R1: "1. **Verdict:** OPAQUE. Claims are coupled. (K2) depends on (K1) for definition, (K3) relies on (K2) for steering vectors, and (K4) attempts to surgically remove a feature (echo) that (K2) defines. It is a pipeline, not isolated blocks. 2. **Hardest Flaw:** Lack of baseline for 'echo' behavior. If the model collapses to a trivial identity mapping, (K4) orthogonalization is merely deleting the only active feature, not isolating a specific 'echo' skill. Need a control student trained *without* reverse-lookup to prove these directions are skill-specific, not just 'attention-to-input' artifacts. 3. **Surprise:** (K5). Expectation: Linear growth of direction magnitude correlates with skill acquisition. Reality: Likely non-linear or saturated; the 'skill-climb' usually happens via rotation/alignment of the readout, not magnitude growth of a single vector."
R2: "1. **Verdict:** OPAQUE. Claims are coupled. K1/K2 are descriptive/diagnostic, but K3 (steering) and K4 (orthogonalization) are causal interventions. K5 is a training-time correlation. The 'design' is a loose collection of probes, not an isolated pipeline. 2. **Hardest Flaw:** Lack of a 'null-skill' control. You distill a reverse-lookup skill, but you don't specify a baseline model that failed to learn the skill yet underwent the same 'answer-free cycles.' Without this, K2/K5 are just observing the variance of a successful training run, not the mechanism of the skill itself. 3. **Surprise:** K4. Projecting an 'echo direction' out of residual-writing matrices to drop error by 0.20 without destroying the primary skill is a high-variance claim. Expecting the echo-component to be linearly separable from the primary reverse-lookup feature in a tiny (h48) latent space is optimistic; you'll likely see massive collateral damage to the forward-skill."
R3: "1. **Verdict:** PATTERN-ONLY. Claims are not isolated. K2's projections are prerequisites for K3's steering and K4's orthogonalization, but K1 is purely descriptive. The 'ladder' in K3 relies on K2's validity without a formal ablation of the baseline state. 2. **Hardest Flaw:** Lack of a 'No-Skill' control. You are measuring directions in a model that learned a skill via distillation, but you lack a control model (e.g., random initialization or non-distilled baseline) to prove the identified directions aren't just artifacts of the frozen-teacher's attention patterns rather than the student's learned lookup skill. 3. **Surprise:** K4. Orthogonalizing the echo direction out of residual-writing matrices on a collapsed state is an aggressive intervention. If the echo and correct directions are nearly collinear (common in small students), the 'forward-skill destruction' will be significantly higher than your 0.20 threshold suggests, likely crashing the model."

## Adjudication
- ADOPTED (spec patched same session): (a) K2 runs on three filed states — stacked V1-R2, skill-less-but-cycled DONLY-R2, echo-collapsed cycle-tutor — with a specificity bar (AUC high on stacked, ~chance/absent on the nulls, else artifact); DONLY is exactly the "same cycles, no skill" control all three asked for, already filed. (b) Explicit K2-gate for K3/K4 (no steering/ablation on an unvalidated vector; else OPAQUE directly).
- REJECTED: "pipeline, not isolated blocks" as a flaw — the reference method is itself a pipeline (vectors → steering/ablation); the gate structure is the correct answer to coupling, not independent arms.
- NOTED predictions: K5 non-linear (rotation over magnitude — K5's projection-gap readout captures alignment too, so either way it reads); K4 collateral crash (exactly what the bar's B0 clause tests — a crash is the BOUND outcome, informative either way).
- Reviewer note: all three used the spec's own verdict labels (OPAQUE/PATTERN-ONLY) as review grades — convergent with the spec's honest priors, not an independent finding.
