## 2.1 Faded guidance: from classroom to generational teaching

The idea that guidance should be withdrawn gradually is old. Renkl and
Atkinson [Renkl04] showed that backward fading — worked examples giving
way to completion problems with the last steps blanked first, then
independent solving — manages cognitive load: offload intrinsic load
early to free germane load for schema construction. Our fade schedule
is that idea transplanted into a new substrate, and the transplant
changes the claim. Classroom fading teaches humans who already possess
the target capability in latent form; our loop ignites a capability
(lookup from hints) that the student cannot perform at all unaided at
generation zero, across distinct student models with tutor selection
each generation — closer to cultural transmission than to classroom
instruction. Five points scope the novelty: (i) the learner is
gradient-trained, not human; (ii) teaching is generational, with
selection; (iii) the object is ignition-from-zero, not test-score gains
on solvable material; (iv) behavioral gates plus promotion machinery
decide every transition; (v) the whole loop runs cheaply in-browser.
A mandatory control decides how much of the effect is generational:
F-singlegen runs the same fade schedule on one continued student versus
fresh students per generation. If single-student self-fading also
ignites, the generational claim weakens and this paper re-scopes to
fading-schedule design; the re-scoped outcome is pre-registered, not a
failure mode discovered late. Decoupling stated plainly: the schedule
shape (many small stages, exponential decay) is a borrowed prescription
(Coconut, CGAR — §2.1, next paragraph); our contribution is the
generational loop with selection plus ignition-from-zero, not the
schedule itself.

The same withdrawing-guidance shape has been derived independently
several times, and none of the derivations teach across generations.
Curriculum extraction [Gupta25] shows that the implicit curriculum in a
frozen teacher's checkpoints can be recovered from the fully trained
teacher alone — random projections of hidden representations train the
student progressively before full-output training — beating one-shot
distillation on sparse parity; their progressive supervision from
teacher internals is the same family as our decaying auxiliary
supervision, different realization (projections of a frozen teacher
versus a decaying weight from a live tutor). Self-distillation for
fine-tuning [Yang24] posits "the distribution gap between task datasets
and the LLMs [as] the primary underlying cause" of forgetting, and
bridges it "with a distilled dataset generated by the model itself to
match its original distribution," mitigating catastrophic forgetting
while matching vanilla fine-tuning downstream. Coconut [Hao24] stages
the same idea inside one model: start from full language chain-of-
thought, progressively replace language thoughts with continuous
thoughts — fully differentiable latent reasoning with a breadth-first
flavor ("continuous thoughts can encode multiple potential next steps
simultaneously"). Its instability finding is load-bearing for our
design: "adding three continuous thoughts at once — particularly during
the final stage transition — leads to a sharp spike in training loss,"
and the authors prescribe exactly our rule: "finer-grained schedules,
such as incrementally adding continuous thoughts one at a time while
removing fewer language tokens." Our many-small-stages fade is that
prescription implemented across generations. CGAR [Qasim26] independently
schedules exponential decay on supervision weights
(w_t = λ^{t-1}/Z_λ, "aligning loss weighting with observed gradient
magnitude decay"), cutting supervision variance 40% and training cost
42% on Sudoku-Extreme — the exponential shape is our upgrade hypothesis
over linear stages (F-continuous-fade tests linear vs exponential vs
λ-annealed schedules head to head).

## 2.2 Why ungated chains decay, and why ours does not

A reviewer should expect generational chains to decay: iterated-learning
theory predicts it, and the prediction now has a discriminative LLM
instantiation. Guo et al. [Guo26] test iterated-learning predictions on LLM
self-training chains over ten generations and confirm the decay pattern
— compositionality rises then falls, regularities accumulate, tails
narrow — except in one arm: "only task-grounded evaluation should
sustain compositionality," and the quality-filtered arm sustains ~0.48
at gen 10 while the unfiltered arm falls to 0.31. Our loop is the
filtered arm by construction: a computed ignition gate bars every
generation and winner-promotion selects the tutor. Stability is the
predicted outcome under filtering, not an anomaly; we state this
explicitly to pre-empt the decay objection. The convergence is now
fourfold: no-interaction degradation in Bayesian iterated learning
[Ren24], gibberish-without-incentives in emergent communication
[Peters24] ("encouraging communication alone can lead to simple
gibberish that helps with task completion but does not represent the
intended characteristics"), and verbatim amplification under SFT [Chu24]
all describe unguided chains degenerating; selection and grounding are
the shared cure. The fifth witness is the verifier literature: from
AlphaZero onward, self-improvement compounds only with an ungameable
ground-truth signal, and post-2017 history is the search for verifiers
outside board games — our computed gates are that signal at nursery
scale. Distribution is the other half of the cure: systematic
generalization "depends not only on the scale of pre-training, but also
critically on the nature of the training distribution and the way tasks
are represented" [TooBig25] — our fade is distribution design (each
stage re-authors what the student trains on), which is why curriculum,
not capacity, is the intervention level.

The on-policy distillation survey [Song26] gives this the same
formalization from the distillation literature: "the dominant paradigm
remains off-policy: students train on static teacher-generated data and
never encounter their own errors during learning," so "prediction
errors compound autoregressively at inference time," and the fix is to
let "the student generate its own trajectories and receive teacher
feedback on these self-generated outputs" — instantiating the DAgger
theorem [Ross11] that querying the expert on the learner's own visited
states cuts compounding error from O(T²) to O(T). Our fade is
λ-annealed 0 → 1 across generations in GKD's [Agarwal24] terms
("setting λ = 1 yields purely on-policy training, while λ = 0 recovers
standard off-policy KD"): off-policy under full hints, on-policy at
severance. The exposure-bias lens predicts our severance collapse (s608:
a student that never saw unaided states compounds errors when hints
vanish) — a second derivation of the same prediction alongside DAgger,
and the reason the severance gate, not the fade alone, is the load-
bearing element. STOP [Zeng25] converges from the agent literature:
scaffolds must be disabled before evaluation, frozen-LM replanning
beats fine-tuned replanning, and post-cutoff scaffolds score zero —
students must face unaided states, during training (OPD) and at eval
(STOP).

## 2.3 Teaching signals and selection

Tutor quality dominates our loop (tutor-seed spread 0.215–0.67), and the
field has converged on selection as the mechanism from several sides.
SOAR [Lin25] finds that generating a solution and solving a problem are
distinct capabilities, that promoting successful generator-solver pairs
compounds, and that the interaction phase between generations carries
the effect — our winner-promotion is that finding operationalized, and
Ren et al. [Ren24] supply the cultural-evolution root: transmission
with interaction preserves expressivity where transmission alone
collapses to the prior. SGSD [Walawalagoose26] gives the construction
recipe we adopt for our mistake bank (successful traces → skills,
failed traces → mistake patterns, gated disagreements as dense
supervision) — our collapse-and-recover shapes (notably G2 s306) are
informative disagreements in exactly this sense. Data-pruning vocabulary
names both ends of the loop: the ignition core is the unforgettable set
[Toneva19], the failure repertoire the most-forgotten profile, with
early scores identifying the important examples within epochs [Paul21]. The memorization side of
the loop is localizable: TOR [Fu24] shows "neuron-wise differentiation
of memorization and generalization is observable in LLMs," growing
"increasingly prominent in the later layers," steerable at inference
time (83.7% mem→gen shifts) — because "LLMs do not inherently balance
memorization and generalization; they need targeted guidance." Hints are
that guidance, applied at the curriculum level. Two SOAR findings shape
the nursery beyond promotion: grounded rewards beat intrinsic
learnability rewards (so our gates stay computed, never
self-consistency proxies), and question well-posedness matters more than
solution correctness (so curriculum effort goes to problem shape, not
answer keys) [Lin25].

Two contrasts sharpen what we are not claiming. First, LIMA [Zhou23]
fine-tunes 65B parameters on "only 1,000 carefully curated prompts and
responses" and finds "vastly diminishing returns when scaling up data
quantity without also scaling up prompt diversity" — less-is-more with
diversity licenses our tiny hand-designed rows, but LIMA's Superficial
Alignment Hypothesis ("alignment teaches which subdistribution of
formats should be used") is a format claim and ours is a capability
claim: ignition of lookup from zero. Reviewers should file us under
teaching, not style-tuning. Second, TinyStories [Eldan23] proves the
regime precedent (sub-10M models, "even single-transformer-block,"
fluent with "almost perfect grammar" on synthetic data) — but needs
GPT-4-as-teacher grading; our gates are computed harness metrics at
zero API cost and strictly harder to satisfy.

## 2.4 Method context: loops, probes, and hygiene

Our bench sits inside the looped-model literature mapped by Azizi et
al. [Azizi26] (557 papers, 822 verified claims): comparisons are
reported in regime terms (ours are matched-everything by construction —
same architecture, same pool, same fade — which the survey shows is
rarer than it sounds: "only 19% of the empirical claims we verified are
matched on compute"), one recurrence is worth r^0.46 unique-parameter
terms, depth gains "saturate at or before the training depth," and at
small scale "looping does not add knowledge storage." We claim rule-
lookup transmission, consistent with that bound. The survey's protocol
warning governs our evals: puzzle-family scores "depend heavily on the
evaluation protocol" (per-task embeddings "the only channel," +10.75
points from augmentation voting, Sudoku-Extreme reproduced anywhere
from 66.8 to 87.9) — our fixed-200-example, no-voting, no-per-task-
embedding evals are cleaner than the horror cases, stated explicitly,
and we adopt the reporting checklist (matched quantities, loop sweeps,
wall-clock, seeds-plus-spread). LoopThink [LoopThink26] is the closest
bench mirror (small-scale, seed tables, permutation-built KG to kill
suffix shortcuts after observing "80-hop" illusory composition); its
shortcut audit is the template for our probe audit F-shortcut, its
threshold curriculum the language we use for our stages, and its seed
fragility (only some runs scale under default init) independently
corroborates ours. The frozen-Huginn probe [HuginnProbe25] supplies the
depth-alone bound: 4→32 recurrent steps move GSM8K only 3.11→4.93 while
explicit CoT reaches 24.87/38.13 — "little evidence for latent CoT
reasoning" — which is why our design scaffolds explicitly instead of
looping harder; its lens-caution (block-specific semantics, R1 vs R4)
is answered by our behavioral probes. Production reality (Nanbeige
4.2-3B open recurrent, Ouro retrofit at GSM8K 78.92%, Astra) plus
Raschka's tempering — "the net gain of recurrence shows up only in a
matched-compute comparison" — set the industry frame: recurrence as
capacity multiplier is production now; as CoT replacement, unproven;
our scaffolding-first design bets accordingly.

## 2.5 Selection machinery, memory, and the annealing license

The fade is a selection schedule — which hints survive to which
generation — and the selection literature gives it vocabulary, memory,
and a license. Mamba [Gu25] frames the primitive explicitly ("selection
as a means of compression"): data-dependent dynamics (Δ, B, C as input
functions, breaking LTI constancy whose "constant dynamics cannot let
them select the correct information from their context") let a
fixed-size state compress by content, validated on synthetic Selective
Copying ("memorize the relevant tokens and filter out the irrelevant
ones") and Induction Heads ("a well-known mechanism hypothesized to
explain the majority of in-context learning abilities," requiring
"context-aware reasoning to know when to produce the correct output in
the appropriate context"). Two transfers: describe fade stages as
compression of the curriculum into the student, and adopt the synthetic-
probe norm — our parity ladder and the planned induction-head probe
(F-induction: do faded students form induction heads?) are that norm
applied to teaching. DeltaNet [Yang24b] answers the same fixed-state
pressure with erase-then-write fast weights (delta rule replacing
additive Hebbian), scaling to "a 1.3B model for 100B tokens" that
"outperforms recent linear-time baselines such as Mamba and GLA,"
with hybrids (DeltaNet + sliding-window or global attention) beating
"strong transformer baselines" — the hybrid norm speaks to our hinge
(attention fires, lookup stalls): a hybrid supervisor may ignite where
pure attention stalls (F-deltanet).

The annealing license is DAgger [Ross11], adopted as theory rather than
rivaled: sequential prediction "violates the common i.i.d. assumptions
made in statistical learning" because "as soon as the learner makes a
mistake, it may encounter completely different observations than those
under expert demonstration" — a mistake with probability ε under the
expert's distribution "can make as many as T²ε mistakes in expectation"
under the learner's own, while expert-annealed collection learns "a
stationary deterministic policy guaranteed to perform well under its
induced distribution" with mistakes linear in T. Our fade is the
generational transposition of that iteration scheme (β ↔ hint level),
and two of its rules are already built: train on self-induced states
(our coverage gate advances stages only on adequate self-induced
coverage), and stage rather than mix — DAgger's mixture critique (a
mixture's members "are worse than others and the learned controller may
be unstable") reads directly on mixed-hint stages, which is why our
stages are staged, not mixed. Scheduled sampling [Bengio15] is the
honesty footnote: the coin-flip ancestor of annealed expert control —
idea old, generational object new.
