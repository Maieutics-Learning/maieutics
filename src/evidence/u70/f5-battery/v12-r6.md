NEEDS-PATCH

1. **Bimodal Blindness / Variance Risk**: Forcing both Mean $\ge X$ and Median $\ge X$ does not actually eliminate the "lucky tail" problem; it simply shifts the requirement to the 50th percentile. A distribution with a massive positive skew (a few extreme outliers) can still pull a mediocre median up to the threshold, or conversely, a few extreme failures can tank the mean while the median remains high. 

2. **Redundancy/Inefficiency**: Because the Mean is sensitive to outliers and the Median is not, requiring both creates a "dead zone" where a run is statistically significant but fails the arbitrary pairing. Specifically, if the distribution is heavily skewed, the pair doesn't provide a meaningful "typical-run" guarantee—it just creates two hurdles for the same underlying variance. It doesn't solve the lottery; it just makes the lottery harder to win.
