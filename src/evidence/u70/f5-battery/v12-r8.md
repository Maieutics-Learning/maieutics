NEEDS-PATCH

1. **Bimodal Blindness / Distributional Conflict**: Forcing both Mean $\ge 0.70$ and Median $\ge 0.70$ creates a "blind spot" for bimodal distributions. A sample could have a very strong cluster (e.g., 21 runs at 0.95) and a very weak cluster (e.g., 19 runs at 0.30). In this case, the Median passes ($\approx 0.95$), but the Mean ($\approx 0.75$) barely scrapes by. Conversely, a distribution with a high mean but a "hole" at the median would fail despite high average performance.

2. **Variance Shrinkage**: By requiring both to pass, you are effectively imposing a hidden constraint on variance. This doesn't just stop "lucky tails"; it penalizes legitimate high-variance systems that are robust on average but occasionally dip, potentially misclassifying "FIXED" systems as "PERSISTS" if the distribution is skewed, regardless of the actual performance magnitude.
