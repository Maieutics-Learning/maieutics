NEEDS-PATCH

1. **Bimodal Blindness / Variance Shrinkage**: The requirement for both Mean $\ge$ X and Median $\ge$ X creates a "dead zone" for distributions with high variance or bimodality. A distribution could have a very high mean (driven by a few extreme outliers) but a median just below the bar, or a high median but a mean dragged down by a few catastrophic failures. By requiring both to pass, you are not actually measuring "central tendency" but rather "absence of catastrophic failure AND absence of lucky-tail dependency."

2. **Median Gaming**: Since the median is derived from the 20th and 21st sorted values of 40, a single run's flip from 0.69 to 0.71 can shift the verdict regardless of the overall distribution's stability, making the "Median" bar surprisingly brittle compared to the "Mean" bar in small samples ($N=40$).
