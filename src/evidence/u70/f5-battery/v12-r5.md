NEEDS-PATCH

1. **Bimodal Blindness / Variance Shrinkage**: By requiring both MEAN and MEDIAN to pass, you create a "dead zone" where a result is penalized for high variance even if the mean is exceptionally high. Conversely, a bimodal distribution (half 0.9, half 0.5) would fail the median check but pass the mean check, potentially masking a "lottery" success that is actually robust in aggregate.

2. **Median Gaming (The "Floor" Problem)**: Because the median is the average of the 20th and 21st sorted values, a researcher can "game" the result by ensuring just over 50% of runs barely clear 0.70, regardless of how disastrous the bottom 49% are. This doesn't actually solve the "lucky tail" problem; it just shifts the requirement from one outlier to a cluster of mediocre runs, potentially ignoring catastrophic failures in the bottom half of the pool.
